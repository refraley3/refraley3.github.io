### [*Symphyotrichum laeve*](/field-notes/plants/angiosperms/dicots/magnoliopsida/asterales/asteraceae/asteroideae/symphyotrichum-laeve/){: .taxonomy-heading .taxonomy-last}

[**Smooth Blue Aster**](/gallery/plants/wildflowers/smooth-blue-aster/) carries blue-violet ray flowers around disks that begin yellow and deepen toward reddish purple with age. For identification, habitat, and natural-history details, see the [*Symphyotrichum laeve*](/field-notes/plants/angiosperms/eudicots/asterales/asteraceae/symphyotrichum-laeve/) species page.

The photographs bring together two encounters. “Morning Jewels” isolates a large, dew-covered flower, while the newer sequence follows a more open branching plant with smaller flower heads, developing buds, and flowers at several stages. The difference in apparent flower size initially raised the possibility of Sky-blue Aster (*Symphyotrichum oolentangiense*). D79A5416 has been identified on iNaturalist as Smooth Blue Aster; the newer E22A9505-16 observation was posted more cautiously as American asters (*Symphyotrichum*) and remains provisional here pending further identification.

Smooth Blue Aster typically has smooth, somewhat waxy leaves whose bases clasp the stem, often with small ear-like lobes. Sky-blue Aster generally has rough-textured leaves that narrow into winged stalks and do not clasp the stem. E22A9530 was made specifically to document that distinction; it should be considered together with the wider habit photographs and the eventual iNaturalist identification.

## Identification Notes

**Working identification:** Smooth Blue Aster (*Symphyotrichum laeve*)  
**Family:** Asteraceae  
**Flowers:** Blue-violet rays surrounding yellow disks that may become reddish with age  
**Leaves:** Smooth to the touch, usually toothless or nearly so, with upper leaves clasping the stem  
**Bloom season:** Late summer into autumn  
**Habitat:** Prairies, open woods, woodland edges, and other sunny to partly shaded sites  
**Similar species:** Sky-blue Aster (*Symphyotrichum oolentangiense*), distinguished especially by rough leaves with nonclasping bases  

The newer observation remains provisional until its diagnostic features and community identification agree. If it is ultimately identified as Sky-blue Aster, the E22A9492–E22A9530 sequence should move to a separate page under *Symphyotrichum oolentangiense*.
