[**Sky-blue Aster**](/gallery/plants/wildflowers/sky-blue-aster/) (*Symphyotrichum oolentangiense*) is a native perennial wildflower recognized by its lavender ray florets and yellow disk florets, which bloom in late summer to early fall.

## Description

Symphyotrichum oolentangiense is a perennial herb that typically grows 2-4 feet tall. It has distinctive lavender ray florets and yellow disk florets. The leaves are lance-shaped and arranged alternately on the stems.

Symbphyotrichum oolentangiense is recognized by:
- **Growth form:** perennial herbaceous plant
- **Height:** typically 2-4 feet tall
- **Leaves:** lance-shaped, alternate arrangement
- **Flowers:** lavender ray florets with yellow disk florets
- **Flower arrangement:** clusters at the top of the stems
- **Fruit arrangement:** small, dry achenes that are dispersed by wind
- **Bloom season:** late summer to early fall

### Soil and Habitat
Symphyotrichum oolentangiense grows in prairies, meadows, and open woodlands. It prefers well-drained soils and can often be found in areas with full sun to partial shade.

## Similar Species
Symphyotrichum oolentangiense can be confused with other asters, such as *Symphyotrichum novi-belgii* (New York Aster) and *Symphyotrichum laeve* (Smooth Aster).

Symphyotrichum oolentangiense is an important nectar source for pollinators, including bees and butterflies. It also provides habitat for various insects and contributes to the biodiversity of native plant communities.
