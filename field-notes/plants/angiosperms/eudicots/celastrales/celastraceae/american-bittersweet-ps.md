### [*Celastrus scandens*](/field-notes/plants/angiosperms/eudicots/celastrales/celastraceae/celastrus-scandens/){: .taxonomy-heading .taxonomy-last}

[**American Bittersweet**](/gallery/plants/vines/american-bittersweet/) is a hardy, native climbing vine known for its striking late-season fruit. For identification, habitat, and natural-history details, see the [*Celastrus scandens*](/field-notes/plants/angiosperms/eudicots/celastrales/celastraceae/celastrus-scandens/) species page.

The tangled stems are both the challenge and the character of this subject. Earlier photographs concentrated on the brilliant opened fruit and twisting woody vines of late autumn. A newly discovered group of vigorous vines now provides an opportunity to follow American Bittersweet through a broader seasonal cycle. E22A9338, E22A9341, and E22A9337 begin that study in late summer, when the capsules have turned orange and the first leaves are yellowing while the surrounding landscape remains predominantly green. Later photographs follow the capsules as they open to expose scarlet arils and, eventually, persist among bare and frost-covered stems.

In autumn, its orange capsules open to reveal scarlet arils surrounding the seeds. The colorful fruit can persist well into winter, providing important color — and wildlife forage — when the landscape becomes subdued. It climbs by **twining** around nearby stems and branches, sometimes forming dense, sculptural tangles. Unlike the invasive **Oriental Bittersweet (*Celastrus orbiculatus*)**, the American species typically bears fruit at the **tips of the vines** rather than along the entire stem.

**Habitat:** Woodland edges, thickets, fence lines, and upland shrub communities across the Upper Midwest  
**Leaves:** Oval, finely toothed, turning yellow before dropping in fall  
**Stems:** Woody, twining vine that climbs by wrapping — not by tendrils  
**Fruit:** Orange capsules that split into three sections, exposing **glossy red arils** in late autumn  
**Color:** Yellow foliage, orange husks, and red fruit — most vibrant after leaf drop
