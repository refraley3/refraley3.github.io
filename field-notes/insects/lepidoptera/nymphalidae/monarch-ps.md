### *Danaus plexippus*
{: .taxonomy-heading .taxonomy-last}

The Monarch photographs in this sequence show the species in several useful contexts: an adult nectaring among tall yellow prairie flowers, a butterfly approaching purple thistle in flight, close views on yellow flowers, and a striped caterpillar on milkweed. Together, the images connect the adult butterfly to its meadow habitat and to the larval stage that depends on milkweed.

For identification details and natural history notes, see the species page: [*Danaus plexippus*](/field-notes/insects/lepidoptera/nymphalidae/danaus-plexippus/).
For the finished image presentation, see the Gallery page: [Monarch](/gallery/animals/insects/butterflies/monarch/).

The adjusted crop of **E21A3459** is the strongest overall adult Monarch image in the set. The butterfly is large enough to read clearly, but the scene still feels open and environmental. The yellow flowers, blue sky, and soft clouds give the image a prairie-summer setting rather than a simple close-up.

The original wider version had a broader meadow feel, but the butterfly was smaller and had to compete more with the surrounding flowers. This revised version keeps the better parts of the wide image while making the Monarch the clear subject.

**E21A5083** adds a different kind of Monarch image. Instead of a perched or nectaring pose, the butterfly is suspended in flight near purple thistle flowers. The open green background helps separate the butterfly, while the thistle stems anchor the left side of the frame.

This image is not as strong as a close identification view, but it is important to the sequence because it adds movement and behavior. It also broadens the floral context beyond the yellow composite flowers shown in the other adult images.

**E21A3466** is the clearest adult identification image in the group. The Monarch fills more of the frame than in the habitat views, and the dorsal wing pattern is easy to see. The black venation, orange wing panels, and white-spotted dark margins are all visible.

The background is busier than in the later crop of E21A3459, but this image remains useful because the butterfly itself is large, sharp, and readable. It provides a good bridge between the more artistic habitat images and the field-identification view.

**D79A0327** belongs in the Monarch sequence because it shows the larval stage on milkweed. Unlike the hairy tussock moth caterpillars reviewed separately, this larva has the smooth black, yellow, and white banding expected for Monarch. The milkweed leaf also helps place the image in the correct biological context.

This photo is not part of the adult nectar sequence, but it gives the page a stronger natural-history connection. It also leaves room to expand the page later if additional images are added for egg, chrysalis, or other larval stages.

## Notes on the Set

The adult Monarch images work best as a small sequence rather than a large collection of similar frames. The adjusted **E21A3459** provides the strongest gallery lead, **E21A5083** adds flight and thistle context, **E21A3466** gives the clearest adult identification view, and **E21A3471** adds a second movement frame.

The caterpillar image should remain separate in tone from the adult images, but it is valuable enough to include because it connects the species page to milkweed and the Monarch life cycle. If more life-cycle images are added later, this page could grow into a fuller sequence: egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, adult, nectaring, and migration-season adults.

The dew-covered hairy caterpillars should not be included here. Those are better treated separately as White-marked Tussock Moth or another tussock moth page, depending on final identification.
