## *Cladonia pyxidata*
{: .taxonomy-heading}

### Family: [Cladoniaceae](/field-notes/lichen/family/cladoniaceae/)
{: .taxonomy-heading .taxonomy-last}

[**Common Pixie-cup**](/gallery/fungi/lichens/common-pixie-cup/) (*Cladonia pyxidata*) is the working identification for the pale gray-green, cup-bearing lichens documented here. **The identification is probable, not confirmed.** The field photographs and the reported microscope view favor this interpretation, but similar cup-forming *Cladonia* remain possible.

For representative images and visual context, see the page: [**Common Pixie-cup**](/gallery/fungi/lichens/common-pixie-cup/) in the **Gallery** section.

## Description

The photographed colony shows:

- **Growth form:** upright cup-bearing stalks among small, scale-like structures at ground level  
- **Color:** pale gray-green, with greener cup interiors  
- **Stalks:** slender to moderately stout podetia of varied heights  
- **Cups:** expanded, open tips, called scyphi, with uneven rims  
- **Surface features:** visibly rough, granular to scaly stalk surfaces  
- **Surroundings:** mosses, low broad-leaved plants, fine stems, and leaf litter  

The revised close-up, E22B1802-12, emphasizes the surface texture and variation in cup shape. E22B1813-26 places the cups within the surrounding vegetation. Its background contains the area featured in the close-up; these are connected views of the same colony.

## Substrate and Habitat

The observed colony was reported growing on fine-textured loamy mineral soil among mosses and low vegetation. Soil pH, precise locality, and exposure were not documented in the material used for this draft.

Published habitat accounts for *C. pyxidata* include soil, mossy rocks, walls, and tree bases, particularly in mildly acidic to mildly base-rich settings. These are general species associations, not measurements of this site. See the [British Lichen Society account](https://britishlichensociety.org.uk/resources/species-accounts/cladonia-pyxidata).

## Identification

**Key features to examine:**

- **Cup and stalk surfaces:** *C. pyxidata* has coarse, corticate granules—granules with an organized outer skin—especially conspicuous inside the cups.  
- **Soredia:** true powdery soredia are absent in the typical species.  
- **Basal squamules:** small, rounded scales are part of the diagnostic combination.  

These characters follow the [British Lichen Society identification account](https://britishlichensociety.org.uk/resources/species-accounts/cladonia-pyxidata), which also cautions that this species is frequently misidentified.

The earlier assessment of the microscope photograph described dense, relatively coarse green granules within a cup, rather than a distinctly powdery covering. Together with the rough surfaces visible in the field images, this supports **probable *Cladonia pyxidata***. However, the photographic evidence does not establish that the granules are corticate or that soredia are absent throughout the specimen.

*Cladonia chlorophaea* remains an alternative because its granular or powdery cup surfaces can be difficult to distinguish photographically. Other similar taxa, including *C. pocillum* and *C. monomorpha*, require attention to basal squamules as well as cups. A specimen-based examination of both structures would strengthen the identification. No chemical test results were supplied for this colony.

## Ecology and Notes

- The colony occupies small openings among mosses and low vegetation on the reported soil substrate.  
- The two stacked photographs provide complementary records: surface detail in E22B1802-12 and surrounding vegetation in E22B1813-26.  
- The microscope view adds useful evidence about cup texture, but its interpretation remains provisional.
