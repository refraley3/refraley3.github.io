### *Cathartes aura*
{: .taxonomy-heading .taxonomy-last}

The [**Turkey Vulture**](/gallery/animals/birds/vultures/turkey-vulture/) photographs collected here show two complementary views of the same bird: a perched individual interrupted after feeding nearby, and a later flight sequence showing the long-winged soaring profile more typical of field encounters. For identification, habitat, and natural-history details, see the [*Cathartes aura*](/field-notes/birds/cathartiformes/cathartidae/cathartes-aura/) species page.

Taken together, the photographs move from close-range detail to field silhouette: bare red head, pale hooked bill, layered dark plumage, long wings, and the shallow V-shaped flight posture that makes Turkey Vulture recognizable at a distance.

## Interrupted Breakfast

The perched photographs show the Turkey Vulture in a less common photographic situation. Instead of circling high overhead, the bird was settled among spring branches after feeding nearby. The interruption left it alert and watchful, with the red head turned toward the observer.

## Soaring Profile

A later encounter added the flight view missing from the perched sequence. In the air, the Turkey Vulture becomes a bird of outline and motion: long wings, spread primaries, a small head, and a gliding posture that separates it from most hawks and eagles before color is visible.

**E22A9237** is the strongest flight frame in this group. The bird is banking enough to show the spread primaries and paler flight feathers, while the red head remains visible against the dark body.

## Photographer’s Perspective

The Turkey Vulture sequence changed once the flight photographs were added. The perched encounter supplied the close detail: red head, pale bill, dark brown feather texture, and the heavy posture of a scavenger resting in the branches. The later soaring frames supplied the field view: long wings, fingered primaries, shallow V, and a bird built to move across broad open air.

The photographs now form a progression:

**interrupted feeding → perched alertness → plumage detail → banking flight → soaring silhouette**

That progression works well for a Photographic Sequence page. The Gallery can remain centered on the strongest perched image, while this page preserves the broader visual context: the same bird as both close-range subject and distant shape in the sky.
