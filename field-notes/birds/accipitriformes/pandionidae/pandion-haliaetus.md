## *Pandion haliaetus*
{: .taxonomy-heading}

### Family: [Pandionidae](/field-notes/birds/accipitriformes/pandionidae/)
{: .taxonomy-heading .taxonomy-last}

[**Osprey**](/gallery/animals/birds/raptors/osprey/) (*Pandion haliaetus*) is a large, long-winged raptor specialized for catching fish. Its white underparts, dark carpal patches, white head with a bold dark eye stripe, and long, angled wings make it one of the most distinctive raptors seen over lakes and rivers in Minnesota.

For representative images and visual context, see the page: [**Osprey**](/gallery/animals/birds/raptors/osprey/) in the **Gallery** section. For photographs documenting the hunting, feeding, flight, and nest observations from Minnregs Lake, see [**Osprey — Photographic Sequence**](/field-notes/birds/accipitriformes/pandionidae/osprey-ps/).

## Description

*Pandion haliaetus* is recognized by:

- **Size:** large raptor with a relatively slender body and long wings
- **Plumage:** dark brown upperparts contrasting strongly with white underparts
- **Head:** mostly white with a broad dark stripe through the eye and extending toward the back of the head
- **Eyes:** yellow to orange-yellow in adults; the dark eye stripe makes the eye especially conspicuous at close range
- **Bill:** strongly hooked, dark bill adapted for tearing fish
- **Wings:** long and comparatively narrow, often held with a noticeable bend at the wrist
- **Underwing:** prominent dark carpal patches contrasting with pale underwing coverts
- **Tail:** moderately long and visibly barred, especially when spread
- **Feet:** powerful feet and strongly curved talons specialized for gripping slippery prey
- **Sex differences:** sexes are similar in plumage; females average larger and may show a more noticeable brown breast band, though this is variable

The overall field impression is of a long-winged, strongly patterned raptor whose white head and underparts contrast sharply with dark wings and eye stripe. In flight, the angled wings can create a shallow M-shaped profile.

## Habitat and Range

Typical habitats include:

- Lakes, rivers, reservoirs, ponds, and large wetlands
- Shorelines and open water with abundant fish
- Dead trees, utility structures, nesting platforms, and other elevated nest sites near water
- Open areas around wetlands where birds can circle, hover, and approach the water unobstructed

Notes may include:

- In Minnesota, Ospreys are most conspicuous during the breeding season and migration.
- Nest sites are commonly placed high above the surrounding landscape, often on artificial platforms or other exposed structures.
- Birds may travel between a nest and nearby fishing areas repeatedly during the day.
- Ospreys often circle over open water while searching for fish and may pause briefly in a hover before diving.

## Identification

**Key features for field diagnosis:**

- **Shape:** long, narrow wings with a distinct bend at the wrist; relatively slender body for a large raptor
- **Head pattern:** white crown and face crossed by a bold dark eye stripe
- **Underparts:** mostly white, contrasting strongly with dark wings
- **Underwing:** dark carpal patches are conspicuous from below
- **Tail:** barred and moderately long
- **Flight:** steady wingbeats mixed with gliding and circling; wings may appear kinked or M-shaped when seen from below
- **Behavior:** strongly associated with open water and frequently seen hunting for fish

The combination of a white head, dark eye stripe, white underparts, dark wrist patches, and long angled wings separates Osprey from most hawks and eagles. Bald Eagles are bulkier with broader wings, while buteos such as Broad-winged Hawk have shorter, broader wings and lack the Osprey's bold dark carpal patches and characteristic head pattern.

## Behavior and Ecology

- Feeds primarily on fish and is highly specialized for capturing prey from the water
- Hunts by circling or gliding over water, sometimes hovering briefly before a dive
- Enters the water feet-first with talons extended toward the prey
- Uses powerful wingbeats to rise from the surface after a strike
- Frequently carries captured fish to a perch or nest area before feeding
- Usually nests close to suitable fishing habitat
- Pairs may occupy large stick nests that are reused and enlarged over successive seasons
- Elevated nest sites provide an open approach and a broad view of the surrounding area

Ospreys are unusually specialized among raptors. Their hunting behavior connects nearly every aspect of their structure and ecology to water: long wings for efficient searching, strong feet and sharply curved talons for capturing fish, and prominent nest sites close to productive feeding areas.

## Notes

- The Minnregs Lake observation began unexpectedly while exploring a trail connection in Murphy-Hanrehan Park.
- The first Osprey was seen circling over the lake before diving into the water. The plunge itself occurred before the camera could be raised, but photographs captured the bird immediately after it emerged.
- The bird successfully caught a fish and later settled on a feeding perch near an Osprey nest farther along the trail.
- A second Osprey was present at the nest and took flight while the first bird remained with the fish.
- After feeding, the first bird moved from the feeding perch toward the nest, producing a short sequence showing raised wings, a spread tail, extended feet, and the transition back to the nest.
- The strongest identification views were made at the nest, where the hooked bill, bright eye, dark eye stripe, white underparts, brown upperparts, barred tail, feet, and talons could be seen in greater detail.
- The photographs document several complementary behaviors during a single visit: circling over the lake, a hunting dive, emergence from the water, successful capture of a fish, feeding, interaction between two birds at the nest site, and movement from the feeding perch back to the nest.
