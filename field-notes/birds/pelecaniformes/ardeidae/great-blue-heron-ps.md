## Photographic Sequence

The rock provides a consistent setting across these four photographs. Reeds, floating leaves, and reflections surround the heron, while changes in its head position alter the outline of the bill and the arrangement of the long feathers. Clouds obscured the sun during the encounter, changing both the illumination and the appearance of the gray plumage.

## White Balance

Clouds obscured the sun during this encounter. ISO was increased from 200 to 400 to compensate for the reduced light while retaining f/8 and 1/250 second. That change addressed the lower illumination; white balance addresses the color of the light.

The earlier rendering of E22B0550 above has a cooler, slightly blue-violet appearance in the plumage. E22B0550-2 below uses the **Cloudy** white-balance setting. The feathers appear more neutral gray, the rock looks warmer, and the greens in the pond are more pronounced. These are two renderings of the same photograph, allowing the color treatment to be compared without a change in the bird's pose.

The histograms show the white-balance. The Cloudy setting is confirmed from the edit, although the white-balance control itself is not visible in this clip. The earlier version's white-balance setting has not been recorded here.

The warmer rendering fits more comfortably alongside the sunlit E22B0531 while preserving the feather separation and clear profile of E22B0550. The comparison illustrates why exposure and white balance should be considered separately: raising ISO compensates for reduced light, while adjusting white balance changes how the colors are rendered.

## Photographic Notes

All four photographs were made with a Canon EOS 5D Mark IV and an EF100-400mm f/4.5-5.6L IS USM lens. Keeping the rock and surrounding pond in the frame preserves the setting while allowing the poses and feather patterns to be compared.

E22B0550 offers the clearest side profile and a strong view of the layered plumage. E22B0531 emphasizes the illuminated breast feathers, E22B0525 adds the backward glance, and E22B0514 introduces the broader setting. Together, they show how a stationary subject can yield distinct photographs through small changes in posture and passing cloud cover.
