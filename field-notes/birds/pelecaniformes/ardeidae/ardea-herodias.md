## *Ardea herodias*
{: .taxonomy-heading}

### Family: [Ardeidae](/field-notes/birds/pelicaniformes/ardeidae/)
{: .taxonomy-heading .taxonomy-last}

[**Great Blue Heron**](/gallery/animals/birds/wading/great-blue-heron/) (*Ardea herodias*) is a large, long-legged wading bird with blue-gray plumage and a pointed bill. Its deliberate movements and tall silhouette make it conspicuous along pond edges and shallow shorelines.

For representative images, see [**Great Blue Heron**](/gallery/animals/birds/wading/great-blue-heron/) in the **Gallery**. For the rocky-perch encounter and a comparison of white-balance treatments, see [**Photography Sequence**](/field-notes/birds/pelicaniformes/ardeidae/great-blue-heron-ps/) in **Field Notes**.

## Description

*Ardea herodias* is recognized by:

- **Size:** Approximately 38–54 inches (97–137 cm) long, with a wingspan of 66–79 inches (167–201 cm).
- **Plumage:** Blue-gray body, a paler head, and a broad black stripe above the eye extending toward the rear of the head. Long plumes give the breast and back a shaggy appearance.
- **Bill:** Long, stout, and sharply pointed; typically yellowish to orange in adults.
- **Structure:** Long legs, a flexible neck, and broad wings. In flight, the neck is usually folded back and the legs extend beyond the tail.
- **Sex differences:** Males and females have similar plumage; these photographs do not establish the bird's sex.

The combination of size, head markings, and folded-neck flight posture is more useful than the apparent shade of blue alone. [Cornell identification guide](https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Great_Blue_Heron/id)

## Habitat and Range

Typical habitats include:

- Shallow margins of lakes, ponds, rivers, and marshes.
- Coastal wetlands and sheltered shorelines.
- Grasslands and agricultural fields used for foraging.

The species is widespread across North America. Northern populations migrate, while some coastal populations remain year-round. [Audubon field guide](https://www.audubon.org/field-guide/bird/great-blue-heron)

In Minnesota, herons return in early spring and breed in colonies, often in trees on islands or floodplains. Most leave for warmer regions as winter approaches. Winter observations occur, but the species is rare or absent across much of the state during that season. [University of Minnesota Season Watch](https://seasonwatch.umn.edu/great-blue-heron)

## Identification

**Key features for field diagnosis:**

- **Shape:** A tall wader that can shorten its apparent neck by drawing the head toward the shoulders.
- **Plumage:** Gray-blue wings and body, contrasting pale and dark head markings, and elongated feathers on the breast and back.
- **Bill:** A substantial, straight point projecting well beyond the face.
- **Voice:** A low, harsh croak, often heard when disturbed or in flight.
- **Behavior:** Slow stalking or prolonged stillness while watching for prey.

**Similar birds:** A Great Egret has white plumage and black legs. A Sandhill Crane flies with its neck extended, whereas a Great Blue Heron normally retracts its neck. A Little Blue Heron is much smaller and lacks the adult Great Blue Heron's contrasting pale head and dark head stripe. See the comparisons in the [Cornell identification guide](https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Great_Blue_Heron/id).

## Behavior and Ecology

- **Feeding:** Usually hunts alone, standing or moving slowly before striking with its bill.
- **Diet:** Fish are important prey, but the diet also includes amphibians, reptiles, insects, small mammals, and birds.
- **Social behavior:** Solitary feeding contrasts with communal nesting. Birds may defend feeding areas against other herons.
- **Nesting:** Builds a stick nest, commonly in a tree within a colony or heronry. Some colonies contain hundreds of nests.
- **Parental care:** Both parents incubate the eggs and feed the young.

Access to feeding habitat and suitable nesting sites shapes the species' use of wetlands and nearby woodland. [Cornell life-history account](https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Great_Blue_Heron/lifehistory)

## Notes

- **Observed encounter:** E22B0514, E22B0525, E22B0531, and E22B0550 show a heron standing on the same rounded boulder. The photographs record changing head positions and feather arrangements; they do not document a prey capture.
- **Age variation:** Immature birds can appear browner and have less-developed plumes than adults. [Cornell identification guide](https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Great_Blue_Heron/id)
- **Light and color:** Passing clouds changed the illumination during this encounter. E22B0550-2 uses Cloudy white balance and appears warmer than the earlier rendering of the same frame. Color differences between these edits should not be interpreted as plumage variation.
