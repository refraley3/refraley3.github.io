## Description

The Wood Duck (*Aix sponsa*) is a medium-sized duck known for its striking and colorful plumage. Males are particularly vibrant, featuring a glossy green head, a white stripe along the side of the face, and a chestnut breast with white spots. Females are more subdued in color, with a brownish body and a distinctive white eye-ring. Both sexes have a long, pointed tail and a slightly curved bill. The Wood Duck is often found in wooded wetlands and is known for its ability to nest in tree cavities.

## Habitat

The Wood Duck (*Aix sponsa*) is typically found in wooded wetlands, including swamps, marshes, and slow-moving streams. It prefers areas with dense vegetation and standing water, which provide both foraging opportunities and nesting sites. The species is known for its ability to nest in tree cavities, often selecting old woodpecker holes or other natural cavities in trees near water bodies.

## Range

The Wood Duck (*Aix sponsa*) is native to North America, with a range that extends from southern Canada through the United States and into northern Mexico. It is commonly found in the eastern and central parts of the United States, as well as along the Pacific Coast. ## Behavior

The Wood Duck (*Aix sponsa*) is known for its secretive and cautious behavior, often staying close to dense vegetation and water bodies. It is an excellent swimmer and diver, capable of foraging for food both on the water surface and underwater. ## Diet

The Wood Duck (*Aix sponsa*) is an omnivorous species, feeding on a variety of plant and animal matter. Its diet includes seeds, grains, aquatic insects, small fish, and crustaceans. ## Reproduction

The Wood Duck (*Aix sponsa*) typically breeds in the spring and early summer. Females lay a clutch of 8 to 14 eggs, which are incubated for about 28 to 35 days. The ducklings are precocial, meaning they are able to swim and feed themselves shortly after hatching.
