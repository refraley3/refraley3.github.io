## Barn Swallow — Photographic Sequence
{: .taxonomy-heading}

### [*Hirundo rustica*](/field-notes/birds/passeriformes/hirundinidae/hirundo-rustica/)
{: .taxonomy-heading .taxonomy-last}

The [**Barn Swallow**](/gallery/animals/birds/perching/barn-swallow/) photographs in this sequence show a juvenile resting on an exposed branch against a clear blue sky. The bird remained on the perch long enough for several changes in posture: quiet profile views, a broad wing extension, and two moments when the wings and body feathers lifted away from the normally streamlined shape.

The yellow gape, warm buff underparts, subdued face color, and relatively short tail streamers are consistent with a juvenile. At rest, the body appears compact and almost neckless. Once the bird begins to stretch and rearrange its feathers, that familiar swallow silhouette changes considerably.

For identification, habitat, and natural-history details, see the [*Hirundo rustica*](/field-notes/birds/passeriformes/hirundinidae/hirundo-rustica/) species page.

## Wing Spread

**E22A8823** is the strongest photograph in the sequence. The extended wing separates into a broad fan of flight feathers while the bird remains anchored to the branch. Unlike the later wing-lift frames, the head is still visible and the brown eye retains enough light to keep the photograph connected to the bird rather than becoming solely a study of wing structure.

The position also shows how much wing is folded into the compact outline of a perched swallow. With the wing opened, the long primaries become the dominant feature of the image.

**E22A8827** places more emphasis on the head. The catchlight is especially distinct, and the upward angle makes the yellow gape prominent. The rounded, slightly puffed body and short tail reinforce the juvenile appearance.

## The Shape Beneath the Feathers

The last two photographs are less conventional portraits, but they reveal something the cleaner perched views conceal.

A resting swallow often appears to have almost no neck. The head, shoulders, breast, and folded wings merge into a compact aerodynamic form. **E22A8867** begins to interrupt that outline as the wings lift away from the body. The photograph is dominated by the movement rather than the face, but it shows how much wing is normally compressed into the resting silhouette.

In **E22A8866**, the change is even more pronounced. The wing is raised, the breast and shoulder feathers are ruffled, and the neck becomes clearly visible. For a moment, the swallow looks much less compact than it does in an ordinary perched posture.

## A Comparison with Tree Swallow

The last two frames recall the Tree Swallow photographs **“Half Open Wing”** and **“Wing Lift.”** The comparison is useful because both species normally present a remarkably smooth, neckless appearance when perched.

In the Tree Swallow image “Half Open Wing,” much of that compact outline remains even while the wing begins to open. “Wing Lift,” however, reveals considerably more neck as the feathers loosen and the bird changes posture.

The Barn Swallow sequence shows much the same transformation. **E22A8867** retains more of the compressed resting shape, while **E22A8866** reveals the neck and separates the ruffled body feathers into distinct layers. The apparent absence of a swallow's neck is therefore less a matter of anatomy than of posture and feather contour: when the bird stretches, lifts a wing, or rearranges its plumage, the structure hidden inside the streamlined silhouette becomes visible.

## Sequence

Taken together, the five photographs move from the structure of an opened wing to conventional portraits and finally to the changing shape of the bird itself:

**Wing Spread → Wing Detail → Bright Eye → Half-Open Wings → Ruffled Wing Lift**

The sequence is a reminder that a perched swallow is not simply a resting version of a bird built for flight. Even on the branch, small movements reveal how wings, neck, body feathers, and tail are compressed into the exceptionally compact form normally seen between flights.
