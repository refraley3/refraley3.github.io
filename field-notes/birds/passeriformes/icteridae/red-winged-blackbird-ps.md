### *Agelaius phoeniceus*
{: .taxonomy-heading .taxonomy-last}

The [**Red-winged Blackbird**](/gallery/animals/birds/perching/red-winged-blackbird/) photographs collected here span several encounters and changing conditions: a brief early spring snowfall, territorial calling from low branches, close-range views of the male's epaulet and plumage, and females moving among grasses and cattails. For identification, habitat, and natural-history details, see the [*Agelaius phoeniceus*](/field-notes/birds/passeriformes/icteridae/agelaius-phoeniceus/) species page.

Taken together, the photographs show two very different visual strategies within the same species. The male announces his presence through exposed perches, voice, posture, and color; the female is more easily absorbed into the vertical pattern and muted tones of the marsh.

## First Spring Snow

The snowfall softened the wetland, muting the background and isolating the male among reeds and cattails. Against that subdued palette, even a partly visible epaulet becomes conspicuous.

## Photographer’s Perspective

The Red-winged Blackbird collection changed as closer encounters accumulated. The snowfall sequence first established the bird within the marsh and gave the page its strongest sense of weather and habitat. Later photographs moved progressively closer: territorial calling, a fully raised epaulet, direct eye contact, and feather structure. The female photographs then broadened the collection again, returning attention to the reeds and grasses while showing a very different plumage pattern and a quieter form of presence.

The photographs now form a progression:

**weather and habitat → territorial display → direct awareness → plumage detail → female voice and concealment**

That progression is why the Gallery can remain selective. The strongest photographs can carry the portfolio there, while this Photographic Sequence preserves the smaller changes in posture, behavior, and setting that connect the individual frames.
