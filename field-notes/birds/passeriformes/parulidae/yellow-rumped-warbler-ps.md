### [*Setophaga coronata*](/field-notes/birds/passeriformes/parulidae/setophaga-coronata/)
{: .taxonomy-heading .taxonomy-last}

The [**Yellow-rumped Warbler**](/gallery/animals/birds/perching/yellow-rumped-warbler/) photographs collected here follow a short autumn encounter as the birds moved through a juniper and nearby branches. The upper crown still held abundant berries, while the lower branches were largely bare. The warblers concentrated their feeding higher in the tree, then returned to more open perches below. For identification, habitat, and natural-history details, see the [*Setophaga coronata*](/field-notes/birds/passeriformes/parulidae/setophaga-coronata/) species page.

The photographs work best as a sequence rather than as isolated portraits. They move from an open perch to active feeding, then to preening and quieter pauses where the bird's eye ring, breast streaking, wing pattern, yellow flank, and yellow rump become easier to study.

---

## Open Sky

**E22B0979** begins the sequence on a bare diagonal branch against open blue sky. The bird is relatively small in the frame, but the clean background and simple perch give the photograph a strong graphic quality.

The yellow flank patch is visible even at this distance, while the streaked breast and pale eye ring remain readable. The open perch also introduces the contrast that became apparent during the encounter: the lower branches were sparse, while the upper portions of the juniper still carried dense clusters of berries.

## Feeding in the Juniper

Higher in the tree, the setting changed completely. Dense sprays of juniper foliage still carried ample berries, and the Yellow-rumped Warblers moved through those upper branches to feed.

**E22B1003** gives the clearest view of the behavior. The bird is framed by fruiting branches with its bill open, making the feeding activity immediately apparent. **E22B1005** places the bird deeper in the foliage and shows how enclosed the upper feeding area could become.

The contrast between the sparse lower limbs and the berry-rich crown became one of the clearest observations from the encounter. The birds were not simply passing through the juniper; they were concentrating their activity where the remaining fruit was most abundant.

## Preening on the Lower Branches

After feeding higher in the tree, the sequence returns to the open lower branches. The uncluttered setting reveals more of the plumage, especially the bright yellow rump that gives the species its common name.

**E22B1012** catches the bird with its head buried into the back feathers. **E22B1013** follows with the eye visible during the preening movement and the yellow rump exposed prominently. In **E22B1014**, the bird settles upright again, leaving the rump and patterned folded wing clearly visible against the sky.

## Among the Branches

Here the value is continuity. The perch and lighting remain nearly constant while the bird changes its attention from the branches below, to a side profile, and finally outward toward the camera.

## On the Lichen Branch

**E22B1030** closes the encounter with the cleanest portrait in the group. The Yellow-rumped Warbler sits fully visible on a lichen-covered branch, separated from the background by a broad field of soft brown and muted vertical grasses.

The eye, streaked breast, folded wing, and yellow flank patch all remain easy to read. Compared with the busier feeding and woodland photographs, the simple background gives the bird more visual weight while the textured branch still preserves a sense of place.

## Photographer’s Perspective

What began as a small set of perched portraits developed into a more complete account of the encounter.

The open lower branches and berry-rich upper crown established the setting. **E22B1003** and **E22B1005** added feeding behavior, while **E22B1012–1014** showed the birds returning to the lower limbs to preen. That sequence also produced the clearest views of the yellow rump. **E22B1018–1024** recorded quieter changes in attention among the branches, and **E22B1030** provided the strongest close portrait.

The photographs now form a progression:

**open perch → feeding → preening → changing attention → clean portrait**

Several of the individual frames stand on their own. **E22B1030** is the strongest close portrait, **E22B0979** the cleanest minimalist composition, and **E22B1014** one of the clearest identification views. The Field Notes page benefits from keeping the less polished frames as well, because the sequence shows how the bird moved through the tree and how behavior revealed details that a single portrait could not.
