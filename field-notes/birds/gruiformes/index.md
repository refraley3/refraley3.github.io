##**Gruiformes** is an order of birds that includes cranes, rails, and limpkins. These birds are characterized by their long legs and necks, and many species are adapted to wetland habitats.

##### Orientation

These notes provide an overview of the order Gruiformes, including key characteristics, habitat preferences, and notable species within the group. It serves as a guide for understanding the diversity and ecological roles of these birds in various environments.

### General Characteristics

- **Structure:** long legs and necks; many species have specialized bills for probing or grasping
- **Flight:** cranes are strong fliers; rails are often secretive and may fly only short distances
- **Voice:** calls vary widely; cranes are known for their loud, trumpeting calls, while rails produce a variety of clucks, grunts, and whistles
- **Behavior:** many species are associated with wetlands; some are migratory, while others are resident

Members of this order occupy a range of habitats, including marshes, swamps, grasslands, and forests, often tied to areas with abundant water and vegetation.

## Field Recognition
When identifying Gruiformes in the field, look for the following key features:
- **Movement:** cranes are often seen walking gracefully in open areas, while rails may be more secretive and difficult to spot
- **Flight style:** cranes have strong, direct flight with long necks extended; rails may fly low and fast through vegetation
- **Posture:** cranes are tall and upright; rails are often crouched and may be hidden in dense vegetation
