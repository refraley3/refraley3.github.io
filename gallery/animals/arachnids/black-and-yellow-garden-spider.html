Argiope aurantia

The Black-and-Yellow Garden Spider is a large, conspicuous orb-weaver of meadows, gardens, prairie edges, and tall herbaceous growth. Often called simply the garden spider, it builds broad circular webs that become especially visible when dew or low sunlight outlines the silk.

This gallery follows the spider through its surroundings and behavior: morning webs, a young hunter among flowers, prey capture, web maintenance, and the smaller male sheltering near the female’s territory. For identification details and comparisons with similar species, see Argiope aurantia in the Field Notes section.

Morning Architecture

Before the meadow fully wakes, dew reveals what daylight often hides. A nearly invisible web becomes a luminous wheel suspended among the grasses, each strand briefly outlined before the morning sun dries the silk once again.

At the Center

The Hunter’s Reward

A grasshopper caught in the orb is quickly immobilized. Before venom is delivered, the female braces against the web and rotates her prey, laying down successive bands of silk until escape is no longer possible. Only then does the meal truly become hers.

Hidden Suitor

While the female commands attention at the center of the orb, the smaller male often remains concealed nearby beneath leaves or along supporting stems. His slender build and muted coloration make him easy to overlook. Remaining close to the female’s web gives him access to her silk while reducing his exposure in the open.
